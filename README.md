**Use this when** someone just said yes and the first week starts now. Paste
the agreement, the proposal, or the thread where they agreed.

## What the first week is for

Three jobs, and they run at the same time. Get what you cannot start without.
Give them something real so the silence does not set in. Put the next
checkpoint on a date before they have to ask for it.

A first week fails in one of two ways. Either you asked for twelve things, got
five, and waited, or you went quiet and they spent the week wondering whether
they made a mistake. Both are scheduling problems, not relationship problems.

## How it works

**1. I read what you actually sold them and quote it.** Every deliverable in
the plan traces to a line in the agreement. If I cannot quote the line, the
deliverable is labeled `GUESSED` and flagged, because the onboarding doc is the
first thing a client treats as a promise and you will be held to it.

**2. I split what you need from them in two.**

| list | meaning |
|---|---|
| `BLOCKS DAY ONE` | you cannot begin without it |
| `NEEDED BY DAY N` | real, but asking now just dilutes the ask |

This split is the whole value. Ask for everything up front and you get a
partial reply and a stalled week.

**3. I name one owner on each side and one channel.** If I do not know who owns
it on their side, that is an open question, not a guess, because it decides who
you chase on Thursday.

**4. I date the week and show you the interrogation.**

## The trap

A naive version writes a generous onboarding plan. It fills the week with
deliverables that sound like what a good agency would do, and two of them were
never sold. The client reads it, reasonably assumes all of it is included, and
now your scope is whatever the AI felt like promising. Hence the quote rule:
no deliverable without a line.

Second trap, the undifferentiated ask. Every asset, login and answer in one
email on day one. It looks organized and it kills the week, because the client
does what anyone does with a twelve item list and sends the easy four.

## Limits

No agreement pasted means nearly everything is guessed, and a guessed
onboarding plan is the exact failure this skill exists to prevent. Paste the
real thing.

It does not know their internal approval chain, their holidays, or how fast
they reply. Dates are proposals until they confirm.

It cannot tell you whether the price was right or whether the scope is
deliverable in the time sold. It plans what you already agreed to.

## How this one knows what it knows

Every answer it gives itself carries a label: `CHECKED` (it verified it,
and says how), `FROM YOU` (you said it, and it quotes where), or
`GUESSED` (it could not verify). Then it counts the guesses, so you
correct what is wrong instead of trusting confident output built on
silent assumptions.

> Never ask for something you can check.
> Never assume something you cannot check.
